One mark for how long and how much it matters

You're right to be suspicious of the clock. A dial reads as a fraction of an hour — which is perfect for 30 minutes and useless for four hours, because there's no honest way to draw "four turns of the same face".

The better news is that this glyph already exists in your codebase. The busyness dots inside the week strip encode duration as width and priority as colour, which is exactly the mark you're describing.

It's already shipping — in the calendar

busyDotWidthClass() maps minutes to a bar width (5 / 9 / 14 / 20 / 28 px), and busyDotClass() colours it by priority — red, amber, indigo, neutral.

So the mark is proven at the smallest size you'll ever need it, and promoting it means a task looks the same in the list, in the editor, and on the calendar. The dots stop needing a legend, because you'd have already learned the glyph on the task itself.

What I'd build

The duration bar (A), with the priority ramp you already ship. Width says how long, fill colour says how much it matters, and it's the only candidate that's already been proven at 5 px inside a calendar cell.

Three refinements over what's in the code today:

  • Re-tune the width ramp. Today's 5/9/14/20/28 spreads evenly across a range where your data isn't. Give the common four bigger steps (6/11/17/24) and let 8h and 16h+ compress (29/33) — spend the resolution where the tasks are.
  • Mark P4 as unset, not as grey. A solid grey fill still reads as a value someone picked. A 1px ring over a faint wash reads as "nothing here". Note it can't be a pure outline — at the 30m width the hollow collapses to a ring with no middle, so the wash is load-bearing, not decoration.
  • Give 16h+ an open right edge. The top bucket is unbounded, so a solid cap lies about it — a broken end says "and beyond" at a glance.

On the clock

Worth keeping for one job: time of day, not duration. A dial is genuinely the right shape for "9:30 AM" and genuinely the wrong one for "4 hours".

The specimen shows exactly where it breaks — 30m and 1h are lovely, and from 2h on it has to give up and print a numeral, at which point you've built a worse version of candidate E.

If you want a shape rather than a bar, candidate B (the segmented capsule) is the one to take: constant footprint, so it never disturbs row alignment, and counting segments is easier than judging length. It costs you the direct time-is-length metaphor and the reuse of the calendar dots.

Colour can't work alone

Whatever ramp wins, priority needs a second channel — roughly 1 in 16 men can't separate a red-orange-yellow ramp, and no ramp survives a greyscale screenshot.

The cheapest redundancy you have is the one already in the editor: the four-bar priority meter. Colour carries it in the list where space is tight; the meter carries it in the editor where there's room for both.

Add a title of "High priority · about 4 hours" on the glyph and the whole thing degrades gracefully to text — which is also what a screen reader gets.
