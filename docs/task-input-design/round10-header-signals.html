05 Just say it

Channel · words

One line next to the project: High priority · about 2 hours. No encoding, no legend, no ramp-up. At P4 the priority clause drops and it reads about 2 hours.

Zero ambiguity, and it's the only option that works for someone who's never seen the app before. It also survives being screenshotted, narrated by a screen reader, or read at 200% zoom.

it's a sentence on a piece of artwork. Nothing about it is delightful, and it competes with the title for the "read me" slot.

06 Priority as weather

Channel · saturation (borrowed)

Priority modulates the whole cover: P1 lights an inner rim and pushes the saturation up, P4 drains it to near-grey. Estimate stays a plain chip.

Included because it's the obvious idea, and because it's worth seeing it fail.

this borrows the channel the project already owns. Flip between P1 and P4 — the project doesn't look lower priority, it looks disabled, or like the app failed to load its colour. Saturation reads as "is this thing on", not "how much does it matter".

Where I'd land

Build02 · Stripe + rail

The only one that gets both variables into the header without borrowing a channel or inviting a misread. The rail's length metaphor matches what an estimate is, and encoding priority by exception means the ordinary task stays calm.

Keep in reserve01 · Meters  ·  05 · Words

01 is the fallback if the stripe reads as chrome rather than signal. 05 is the accessible layer underneath whichever visual encoding wins — it should exist as the title and aria-label regardless.

Don't03 · Gauge  ·  06 · Saturation

Both are prettier than 02 and both mean the wrong thing. An arc says progress; a drained colour says broken.

The rule underneath

One variable, one channel. The project already spends hue and texture. Any signal that reaches for those two is going to read as "something about the project", not "something about the task".

That's also why priority must not take its colour from the project. Red has to mean urgent in Health exactly as it does in Home — the specimens all keep the priority ramp fixed at red / amber / indigo / grey for that reason.

Which surfaces the collision from round 9 again: with the Home project active, the amber P2 stripe and the amber project cover are the same colour. Either priority drops amber for a distinct ramp, or the stripe needs a hue-distance check against the cover.

Worth asking first

Does priority belong up here at all? On a personal list the questions are "when" and "how long". Priority is the field most likely to sit at its default forever — which is precisely the argument for encoding it by exception rather than giving it permanent furniture.

Estimate is the stronger candidate of the two: it's the number that decides whether a task fits in the gap before dinner, and it's the one the week strip's busyness dots are already sized by. Putting estimate in the header and leaving priority in the body is a legitimate fourth answer — specimen 02 degrades to exactly that at P3 and P4.
