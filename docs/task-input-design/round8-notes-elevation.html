Getting the notes above the fold

Today the notes sit last, under a calendar that owns roughly half the modal's height — on a 640 px × 90vh editor you scroll to reach them. Four ways to fix that, all borrowing Todoist's move of demoting metadata to a rail so the writing surface can come first.

Every mockup uses the same task and the same components you already ship — the week strip with its busyness dots, the priority and estimate meters, the linkified notes box. Only the arrangement changes.

Click the Scheduled row to see the calendar popover

What it does

  • Notes start at ~130 px from the top — second thing you read, right under the title.
  • Every property becomes one scannable row; the rail reads top-down as when → deadline → where → how much.
  • The calendar keeps all its behaviour — busyness dots, the runway wave, month scroll — it just opens on demand.

What it costs

  • Picking a date is two clicks instead of one. That's the trade the whole option turns on — dating is the most frequent edit here.
  • The busyness dots stop being ambient. You only see how loaded a day is once you've opened the picker.
  • Needs a real popover layer with collision handling; the rail sits at the modal's right edge.

Click the date chip to see the calendar popover

What it does

  • Notes start at ~150 px, and the whole editor fits above the fold at 680 px wide — no side-scrolling, no breakpoint work.
  • The chip row is the same vocabulary as the quick-add bar and the mobile composer, so one control language covers capture and edit.
  • Cheapest of the four to build: the chips already exist in quick-add-chips.tsx.

What it costs

  • Everything is behind a popover — eight of them, all opening downward over the notes.
  • Priority and estimate lose their meters to a 14 px chip glyph; the "how loaded is Thursday" read disappears entirely.
  • The chip row wraps to two lines on a long project name, which shoves the notes back down.

Click Schedule / Details to switch the right pane

What it does

  • Notes become the tallest thing on screen — a real writing pane, ~300 px before it scrolls.
  • The Schedule tab gets more room than today, not less: two full weeks visible without expanding.
  • Clean split of concerns — the left pane is what the task is, the right is what the system knows about it.

What it costs

  • A tab hides half the metadata. Priority and project stop being glanceable, and there's no badge telling you what's set behind Details.
  • Tabs inside a modal are a second navigation layer — the modal already drills into subtasks and climbs to parents.
  • Most work of the four: new pane component, tab state, and a decision about which tab opens by default.

Where I'd land

  • B, then A as the narrow-viewport fallback. B is the only one that lifts the notes without taxing the single most frequent edit in this modal — picking a day — and it keeps the busyness dots doing their ambient job.
  • A and B share a component: the rail is the same set of rows, B just mounts the week strip as its header. Building B gets you A for the sub-1040 px case almost free.
  • C is the one to take if the modal should stay one column on phones too — it's the same layout the mobile composer already uses.
  • D reads best in a screenshot and worst in use; a tab that hides priority behind a click is a regression from today's always-visible 4-up.
